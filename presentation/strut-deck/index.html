Functional Analytic Psychotherapy

Using the therapeutic relationship itself to create change

Awareness · Courage · Love

What is FAP?

A behavioral psychotherapy developed by Robert Kohlenberg and Mavis Tsai (1991).

Its core premise: meaningful change happens through a genuine, intense, and curative therapeutic relationship.

Clinically Relevant Behaviors

The client's problems show up live, in the room, with the therapist.

  • CRB1 — problem behaviors as they occur in session
  • CRB2 — improvements that occur in session
  • CRB3 — the client's own talk about their behavior

The Five Rules

  • 1 · Be aware — watch for CRBs
  • 2 · Be courageous — evoke CRBs
  • 3 · Be therapeutically loving — naturally reinforce CRB2s
  • 4 · Be aware of your impact on the client
  • 5 · Interpret functionally & generalize to daily life

Natural reinforcement

Change is driven by genuine, contingent responding to in-session improvements — not arbitrary praise.

The therapist reacts as caring people would in the client's real life.

The ACL model

  • Awareness — notice what matters, in yourself and the other
  • Courage — risk emotional exposure and vulnerability
  • Love — respond with genuine care and connection

Why it matters

By working with behavior as it unfolds in the relationship, FAP builds new interpersonal repertoires that the client can carry into life outside the therapy room.

The relationship is the treatment.
